A console-level ban or suspension is invisible until you actually try to sign in — and unlike most problems with a used device, it doesn't belong to the previous owner's account. It belongs to the hardware. If a PS5 or Xbox was suspended from its network before you bought it, that restriction typically travels with the console to you, and neither Sony nor Microsoft will generally reverse it for a new buyer. That's what makes this worth checking before you pay, not after.

## Before you buy

Ask the seller for a live video, recorded now: powering the console on, signing into their account (or a fresh one), and reaching the store or online multiplayer. A seller who says "works fine" but won't do this small, specific thing is the actual warning sign — more than anything else in the listing.

## After you have it in hand

1. **Try signing in** on the console itself and note any error code — on PlayStation, a code like WS-37338-4 points at a console-level suspension, not a normal login issue.
2. **Test the same account on a different device** — a phone or PC for PSN, another Xbox or a browser for Xbox. If it works everywhere except this one console, the console itself is the problem.
3. **Check for a suspension notice** in the email tied to the account (including spam/junk).
4. **Check official support status** — PlayStation's My Support page, or Microsoft's Xbox Enforcement page — for the account or device's current standing.

If a ban shows up after you've paid and the listing didn't disclose it, asking for a return is generally more realistic than trying to get the restriction lifted.
