If you're buying something locally and meeting the seller in person, you already have the one advantage that no amount of listing photos or seller assurances can replace: you can test the actual device, with your own hands, before any money changes hands. That's worth using deliberately, not just glancing at the item and handing over cash.

## Before you go

- Agree on a public, well-lit meeting place — a coffee shop or a busy retail parking lot, not somewhere isolated.
- Ask the seller to have the device charged and ready to power on when you arrive — you want to test it, not watch it boot for the first time in a week.
- If it's a device with a known common failure mode (a console's analog sticks, a camera's zoom lens, a laptop's battery and screen), decide beforehand what you're going to check, so you're not improvising under time pressure.

## At the meetup

Power it on. Run through the one or two things that actually tend to fail on that specific device — not a generic glance, the actual check (stick drift on a controller, zoom-and-photograph for a camera, sign-in and network access for a console). Only pay once you've seen it work, not before.

## Where this doesn't apply

None of this helps if you're buying something that's shipping to you instead of meeting up — which is now the more common way people buy used electronics, and the one case where you genuinely can't do any of the above yourself before paying. That's a different problem with a different answer (having the seller run the same kind of check on camera, before you commit, is what TestPass is built for) — but if you can meet up in person, you don't need a tool for this. You just need to actually do the check.
